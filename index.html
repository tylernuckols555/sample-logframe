Human-elephant coexistence through agroforestry: results chain

ZSL Thailand, Kanchanaburi Province (southern Western Forest Complex, sWEFCOM) · Darwin Initiative 30-003

Farmers in Kanchanaburi rely on elephant-attractive crops for income. Wild elephants raid those crops: cassava is grown across all 17 surveyed communities and loses a mean 42% to elephants, government compensation covers roughly 10% of the loss, and a single raid can eliminate a season's income. The chain below sets out how diversified, elephant-friendly agroforestry is intended to change that. The band beneath it sets out the social safeguards that determine whether the change is equitable, not only aggregate.

Reading this chain. It follows the Conservation Standards causal sequence: action → result → threat reduction → focal value, with assumptions sitting on the links and a goal or objective attached to key results. The Outputs tier is the programme's Darwin logframe products, shown here as the earliest results so donor and Open Standards logic read as one diagram.

Enabling condition: social safeguards (ZSL FAIRER / GEDSI)

These conditions determine whether the chain above delivers equitable outcomes, not only aggregate ones. The programme's GEDSI synthesis maps its safeguards to the environmental-justice dimensions below: whether the costs and benefits of living with elephants are recognised, whether procedures are fair, and whether benefits are equitably distributed.

Framing follows the environmental-justice account used in the programme's GEDSI synthesis (Schlosberg, 2007; Moser gender-planning framework; Kabeer). Recognition failures normalise distributive failures and let procedural failures go unchallenged (Martin et al., 2016). The fourth dimension, capabilities, is tracked through the household Wellbeing Index.

Situation model: the current state. Open Standards practice builds a results chain by converting a situation model. Where the results chain shows the desired future state ("how we will change the world"), the situation model shows the present ("how the world currently works"): how governance and climate drivers produce direct threats, and what is at stake. Drivers are policy and market conditions, not only "conflict".

Scope note: the concept note frames the programme around two species, the Asian elephant and the bumblebee bat (Craseonycteris thonglongyai). This results chain and situation model address the elephant focal value; the bat is out of scope here.
